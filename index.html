How to read it

  • The axes follow the Political Compass. Horizontal is economics: state ownership, redistribution and public provision on the left; free markets and private enterprise on the right. Vertical is authority, kept separate from economics: one-party rule, censorship, the death penalty, nationalism and enforced moral codes at the top; civil liberties and limits on state power at the bottom.
  • Authority has no side. Backing the Soviet Union or Castro's Cuba counts as authoritarian exactly as backing an ethno-nationalist party does. A big public sector on its own does not: Nordic-style social democrats sit lib-left.
  • Actions outweigh words. Endorsements, donations, votes and offices held count most, then sustained activism, then self-description, then interviews. Lyrics count least. Being offensive is not the same as being authoritarian.
  • Who is included: people who are either widely known or have a substantial political record, up to 200. Genre is shown by colour and shape together, and is a filing decision, not a claim.
  • Precision: high-confidence marks are good to about ±1 unit, medium ±1.5, low ±2.5. Crowded areas collapse into numbered groups; tap one to open it.
  • Sourcing: placements marked not yet sourced come from general knowledge and are being checked one by one. Anything that cannot be sourced will be removed.
  • Limits: the sample is hand-picked and mostly Anglo-American. The Political Compass itself is criticised for its method and some placements; this chart borrows its axes, not its test or its rankings.
